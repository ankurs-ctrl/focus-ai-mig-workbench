## Required corrections to your plan
1. PROMOTE cache correctness to the FIRST code change, ahead of parallelization.
   Your step 7 assumes FIR recommendations are normally cache-served; the evidence
   shows the prepared-context durable tier holds 4 stale rows while the proc ran 39
   times in 3 days. Parallelizing redundant calls still executes them. Deliver first:
   (a) bound-parameter JSON writes for durable cache persistence,
   (b) loud logging/metrics on cache-persist failures (never silently treat as a miss),
   (c) measured cache hit-rate reporting,
   (d) de-duplication of the FIR/learning/semantic retrieval call sites so one
       selection computes context once and reuses it across prepare, signal
       evaluation, and agent context assembly.
2. TEMPER the parallelization expectation. With your cap of 4 concurrent operations
   per request and ~11 independent learning queries (~3 waves, and any wave containing
   the 6-11s proc is gated by it), cold learning improves roughly 16s -> 6-9s, not to
   ~2s. Keep your ">=50% cold prepared-context improvement" gate; state the expected
   range explicitly so nobody expects more. Your single-cluster Medium decision is
   correct precisely because parallelism is bounded — keep it.

## Required additions
3. Fix the procedure internals, not just the call site. SP_GET_TABLE_RELATIONSHIPS
   begins with `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.DATABASES` plus two more
   existence checks. ACCOUNT_USAGE is multi-second. Replace with a cheap
   INFORMATION_SCHEMA/SHOW probe or drop the guards and let SHOW ... KEYS fail
   gracefully. Needed because your negative-capability cache will still call the proc
   in environments where FK evidence may exist.
4. Make the 29% low-confidence blind spot an explicit, tracked product decision:
   either surface low-confidence joins with a "needs review" badge, or formally accept
   that those tables show no relationships. Do not let a performance plan silently
   ratify the gap. Keep the confidence rules themselves unchanged (as you proposed).
5. Add a workstream for the durable conversation-memory under-persistence (16 turns
   since June). FIR learns from turns, feedback, and corrections, so a lossy capture
   layer starves the learning loop we are about to invest in. Treat as data-quality,
   not performance, and fix before layering more FIR learnings on top.
6. Add the dead-route/persistence gap: /sttm/builder/[id] is a stub returning <></>,
   all navigation goes to /sttm/builder/new, and draft identity survives only via a
   localStorage pointer (lost in incognito / cleared storage / another browser).
   Recommend a durable STTM id in the URL with load-by-id on mount, and earlier
   server-side autosave for brand-new drafts. Robustness, low priority vs latency.
7. Note that removing the coco-agent container does NOT shrink the image: the Cortex
   CLI (cortex.tar.gz) is baked into the sttm-builder image, so cold image pulls stay
   slow. Recommend gating it behind a build ARG to cut cold-start time.

## Required adjustments for FIR data growth (development environment)
8. Treat today's procedure timings as a FLOOR, not a ceiling: 6.3s avg was measured on
   a nearly empty dataset. Cost will grow with FIR volume, so the caching and query-shape
   fixes are more urgent, not less.
9. Make FIR query predicates prunable NOW (push project/target/trigger/context/status
   filters ahead of FLATTEN, joins, and ranking). Keep deferring physical clustering /

   since retrofitting later is more expensive.
10. Invalidate the FIR/learning cache NARROWLY (per target identity, context hash, and

    ingestion, a coarse epoch bump would invalidate everything and reinstate the slow
    procedure on every turn.
11. Make benchmarks and the credit gate ROLLING: re-baseline periodically against the
    current build rather than validating once against a frozen number.
